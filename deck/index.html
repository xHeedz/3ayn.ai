The problem

None of the existing tools can tell you someone is approaching.

Hardware, sensed

A cane that stays silent until silence would be a lie

3AYN-CN-02 · schematic, not to scale

Held at 45° in a two-point sweep, so the tip reads the ground and nothing else. The ultrasonic head is aimed forward at chest height to catch what a sweep structurally cannot: overhangs, open car doors, a person standing still.

Haptic vocabulary

Obstacle ahead, closingUpper
Drop-off or descending stepBoth
Person approaching (from glasses)Lower
Hand extended toward the wearerUpper
Pairing lostLower

Five patterns only, and none of them continuous. A cane that buzzes constantly is a cane that gets switched off — silence is the resting state, and the wearer's own hearing is never competed with.

What's next

The roadmap beyond this submission

01Currency identification, so a Lebanese banknote is recognised by sight, not guessed.
02Object memory — “where did I leave my keys” answered from what the camera already saw.
03Lebanese dialect tuning, so replies sound like family, not a textbook.
04An on-device obstacle model, for a fully offline safety loop.
05The wearable kit built out from spec to working electronics.

Sequenced by user value, not novelty

Currency comes first because it is the request we heard most often and the one a sighted stranger is asked for most. The offline obstacle model comes fourth because it is the hardest and the least visible — and shipping it early would mean shipping it badly.

The sensing envelope on slide 11 is already specified. Items 01–03 need no new hardware at all.
